Crabgrass is a summer annual. It dies every fall and comes back from seed every spring. A preventer (also called a pre-emergent) makes a thin barrier in the top of the soil that stops those seeds from making it. It doesn't kill crabgrass you can already see.

So timing is the whole game. Too early and the barrier wears off before crabgrass is done sprouting. Too late and the crabgrass is already up.

## How to Tell the Soil Is Ready

- **The thermometer way:** crabgrass starts sprouting when the top couple inches of soil stay around 55°F for several days. The [live soil thermometer](/) on my homepage shows today's reading for your ZIP.
- **The forsythia way:** those yellow shrubs bloom right about when the soil hits that range. When they're in full bloom and starting to fade, you're in the window.
- **The calendar way (least accurate):** early to mid April in most of the Northeast. I put it on my calendar for Tax Day as a reminder, then check the soil.

## How to Apply It

1. Mow and clear leaves first so the granules reach the soil.
2. Use a spreader at the rate on the bag. Go around the edges first, then fill in. [Spreader tips](/blog/spreader-settings-half-rate-twice/).
3. Hit the hot spots: along driveways, sidewalks and the street, where the soil warms first and crabgrass loves it.
4. Water it in (about half an inch) within a few days, or time it before rain.
5. Don't aerate or dethatch afterward. Breaking up the soil breaks the barrier.

## Missed It?

- **Early May:** apply anyway. Some crabgrass is still on the way, especially in cooler spots.
- **Crabgrass already up and small:** a product with dithiopyr can stop very young crabgrass, or use a crabgrass killer on the plants you see. [Pre or post?](/blog/pre-emergent-vs-post-emergent/)
- **It's August and it's everywhere:** don't spray it. It dies at the first frost. Pull the clumps, then overseed those spots in September so grass fills the gaps. Next April, set the reminder.

## The Long-Term Fix

Crabgrass needs sun on bare soil. A thick lawn [mowed at 3.5 to 4 inches](/guides/how-high-to-mow-grass/) gives it neither. Most people who mow tall for two seasons stop needing a preventer at all.
