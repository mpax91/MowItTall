Weed killers don't know the difference between a weed seedling and the grass you just paid for. That's the whole problem.

## The Two Ways Weed Killer Wrecks New Seed

**1. Preventers stop seed from sprouting.** Crabgrass preventer (a pre-emergent) sits in the top layer of soil and stops any seed from coming up. Crabgrass, sure. Also your grass seed. Most of them last two to four months, so a preventer in April can still block seed in June.

**2. Sprays burn baby grass.** Dandelion and clover sprays (post-emergents) kill what they touch. Established grass shrugs it off. Grass that's two weeks old doesn't.

## How Long to Wait

These are typical ranges. **The label on your product always wins.**

| You used | Before seeding | On new grass |
|---|---|---|
| Regular crabgrass preventer | Usually 2 to 4 months | Wait until it's been mowed 2 to 3 times, often longer |
| Broadleaf weed spray (dandelions, clover) | Usually 2 to 4 weeks | After 3 or 4 mows |
| Kill-everything spray (non-selective) | Often about a week | Don't. It kills grass too |
| Mesotrione (Tenacity) | Same day is fine | After 2 mows or about 4 weeks |
| Siduron | Same day is fine | Per the label |

## The Exception: Mesotrione

Mesotrione is the one ingredient most homeowners can find that you can put down **the same day you seed**. It stops crabgrass and a bunch of broadleaf weeds from sprouting, and it doesn't stop cool-season grass seed. It's sold as Tenacity.

Know three things before you buy it:

- **Timing matters.** Put it down before the new grass comes up. Once the seedlings are up, wait until they've been mowed twice or about four weeks.
- **Weeds turn white** before they die. The lawn can look a little bleached for a week or two. That's it working.
- **It's not for bentgrass.** It will bleach and damage it.

Siduron is the other seed-safe preventer. It's mostly sold mixed into starter fertilizer, and it only handles crabgrass, not broadleaf weeds.

## What This Means for Your Calendar

- **Seeding in September?** Skip weed killer on those spots this fall. Spray the survivors next spring, once the new grass has been mowed a few times. Spot-spraying old, established grass well away from the new stuff in October is fine.
- **Seeding in spring?** Skip the regular crabgrass preventer on those spots, or use mesotrione. And read [why I don't recommend spring seeding](/blog/spring-seeding/) first.
- **Want weeds gone and new seed in one fall?** Spray in late August, wait the label time, seed in September. [Weeds or seed first?](/blog/fall-kill-weeds-or-plant-seed/)

**Bottom line:** grass first, weeds second. Thick grass crowds out most weeds by itself.
