Every spring, the strip of lawn along the driveway and the street looks like it lost a fight. It did. With salt.

## Why Salt Kills Grass

Salt builds up in the soil as snow melts and runs off the pavement. Salty soil makes it hard for roots to take up water, so the grass dries out even when the ground is wet. Damage usually shows up within a few feet of roads, driveways and sidewalks.

## How to Use Less

1. **Shovel first.** The less snow, the less melt you need.
2. **Spread thin.** Leave space between the grains. A 12-ounce coffee mug of salt is enough for a 20-foot driveway or about 10 sidewalk squares.
3. **Don't toss handfuls.** Clumps of salt make burned spots, same as spilling fertilizer.
4. **Use sand or grit for traction** when it's too cold for melt to work anyway.

## Which Melt to Buy

- **Rock salt (sodium chloride):** cheapest, hardest on lawns and plants.
- **Calcium chloride:** works at colder temperatures, so you use less. Easier on grass than rock salt.
- **Calcium magnesium acetate (CMA):** gentler on plants and concrete, costs more.
- **Potassium chloride:** also used as a fertilizer, but it's still a salt and can burn grass.
- **Sand:** no melting, no salt, just traction.

## Protect the Lawn

- **Pile snow on the pavement side,** not on the grass, when you can.
- **Mark the driveway edge** with stakes so the plow (and you) don't scrape the turf.
- **Ask the plow guy** to keep salty piles off the lawn. Polite, specific, one time.

## Spring Fix

1. **Flush it.** Once the ground thaws, water the salty strips deeply a few times to push salt down past the roots.
2. **Rake out dead grass** during [spring cleanup](/guides/spring-lawn-cleanup-checklist/).
3. **Reseed in September,** not spring. [Fix bare spots](/guides/fix-bare-spots-in-lawn/) the right way.
4. **Gypsum?** Sometimes suggested, but it only helps where water drains well, and roadside soil often doesn't. A soil test first.
