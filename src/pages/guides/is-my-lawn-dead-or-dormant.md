Every August, someone on Nextdoor posts a photo of a brown lawn with the caption "is it dead?" It almost never is. It's napping.

## Why Lawns Go Brown in Summer

Northeast lawns are cool-season grass: bluegrass, rye and fescue. When it gets hot and dry, they shut down on purpose. The blades go brown, but the crowns and roots stay alive, waiting for rain. That's dormancy, and it's normal.

## The Tug Test

Grab a handful of brown grass and pull.

- **Holds on:** dormant. It'll come back.
- **Pulls out easily, no roots:** dead.
- **Peels up like carpet:** probably [grubs](/guides/grubs-in-lawn/), not drought.

Other clues: dormant lawns brown out evenly across sunny areas. Dead or diseased spots tend to have sharper edges or odd shapes. Not sure? [Tap what you see](/resources/whats-wrong-with-my-lawn/).

## How to Keep a Dormant Lawn Alive

- **Pick a lane.** Water enough to keep it green all summer, or let it go dormant. Don't flip-flop.
- **In a long drought, about an inch of water a month** keeps the crowns alive. Grass can usually handle about 4–6 weeks of dormancy; beyond that, you start losing it.
- **Stay off it.** Dormant grass breaks under feet and tires.
- **Don't fertilize.** It can't use it.
- **Don't spray weed killer.** Herbicides on drought-stressed grass in the heat can injure it.
- **Mow rarely, if at all,** and at your highest setting.

## When the Rain Comes Back

Dormant grass greens up in a week or two after a good soaking rain. Give it time before you panic.

## If It Really Died

Mark the dead spots and [reseed them in September](/guides/how-to-overseed-lawn/). That's the best time to grow grass in the Northeast. If your lawn browns out every summer and you never water, [tall fescue](/blog/grass-types-northeast/) is the grass to seed. It has the deepest roots of the bunch.
