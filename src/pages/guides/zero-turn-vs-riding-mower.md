Both of these are sit-down mowers. The difference is how they steer, and that one difference decides almost everything else.

## How They Work

- **Lawn tractor (riding mower):** a steering wheel turns the front wheels, like a car. Engine up front, deck underneath.
- **Zero-turn:** two levers (or a steering wheel on some newer models) drive each rear wheel separately. Push one forward and pull one back and it spins in place. That's the "zero turn."

## Side by Side

| | Zero-turn | Lawn tractor |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Faster, often a lot | Slower |
| Tight turns, trees, beds | Excellent | Wide turns, more trimming |
| Hills | Poor; stay on gentle slopes | Better (still within the maker's limit) |
| Towing, plowing, carts | Limited | Built for it |
| Learning curve | A day of jerky lap-bar driving | None; it's a steering wheel |
| Price | Usually more | Usually less |

## Pick a Zero-Turn If…

- You have a big, mostly flat lawn (roughly an acre or more) with lots of trees, beds and curves.
- Time matters more than towing.
- You like straight passes and crisp stripes.

## Pick a Lawn Tractor If…

- You have real slopes.
- You want to tow a dump cart, pull a spreader or aerator, or put a plow or blower on it in winter.
- You want simple steering and a lower price.

## The Hill Rule

This is the one that matters. Zero-turns steer with the rear wheels, so on a slope they can lose traction and slide. Industry safety guidance for zero-turns commonly says to stay off slopes steeper than about 15 degrees, and to mow up and down slopes rather than across with sit-down mowers. Your model's manual has the real limit, and it always wins. If a section is steep, mow it with a walk-behind, across the slope.

## The Honest Answer

Under about half an acre, skip both. A good [self-propelled walk-behind](/guides/self-propelled-vs-push-mower/) is cheaper, easier to store, and still a [workout](/blog/mowing-the-lawn-is-a-workout/). Between half an acre and two acres, a lawn tractor fits most Northeast yards. Big and open: zero-turn. Not sure how big yours is? [Measure it on a map](/measure/) in a minute.
