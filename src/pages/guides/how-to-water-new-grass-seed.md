You put down seed. Now you stare at dirt every morning. Same. Here's exactly how to water while you wait.

## The One Rule

**Keep the top inch of soil damp.** Not soaked. Not dry. Damp. Seed that sprouts and then dries out can die, and that's the number one reason fall seeding fails.

## Week by Week

| When | How often | How |
|---|---|---|
| Seed day to sprouting (about 1–2 weeks) | Once or twice a day; 3x on hot, windy days | Light: just dampen the surface |
| Sprouted, growing in (weeks 2–4) | Once a day, then every other day | A little longer each time |
| After the first few mows | Like the rest of the lawn | Deep, about an inch a week, rain included |

Bluegrass is slow. It can take three or four weeks to show up, so keep watering. [Track it day by day](/resources/can-i-seed-now/#seedwatch).

## How to Water Without Washing It Away

- **Use a gentle sprinkler or a shower nozzle,** not a jet.
- **Stop before puddles form.** Puddles mean you're moving seed.
- **Slopes:** shorter, more frequent waterings so it soaks in instead of running off.
- **Rain counts.** A soaking rain is a free watering. Check [should I water?](/resources/should-i-water-my-lawn/) before you drag the hose out.
- **Evening is OK in fall** for new seed: cool nights mean less disease risk, and it stays damp longer.

## Things That Undo All Your Watering

- **Walking on it.** Keep people and dogs off until you've mowed it once or twice.
- **Weed killer.** Not on new grass until it's been mowed three or four times.
- **Mowing too soon.** Wait until the new grass is about 4 inches tall, then mow at 3.5 inches with a sharp blade.

Sprinkler system? Make a separate seeding program: short runs once or twice a day. Switch back to your [normal settings](/guides/how-long-to-run-sprinklers/) once the grass is established.
