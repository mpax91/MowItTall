Lots of people park the mower when the leaves start falling. That's early. Cool-season grass keeps growing through October and well into November around here, and fall is when it's building roots for next year.

## How to Know You're Done

- **It stopped growing.** A week or more goes by and it looks the same height. That's the real signal.
- **Highs stay under about 50°F.** Around Bedford that's usually mid to late November. The [live soil thermometer](/) shows when the soil drops into the 40s.
- **Not because of a date.** A warm November means more mowing. A cold one means less.

## The Last Mow, Step by Step

1. **Step the height down.** Over your last two mows, drop from your summer height (3.5 to 4 inches) to about 2.5 to 3 inches. Never cut more than a third at once. [Mowing height guide](/guides/how-high-to-mow-grass/).
2. **Mulch the leaves.** Mow right over them. Chopped leaves are free food. If they're piled deep, bag some first.
3. **Mow when it's dry.** Wet November grass clumps and tears.
4. **Optional late feeding.** Some people put down one last fertilizer after the grass stops growing but while it's still green. [When to fertilize](/guides/when-to-fertilize-lawn/).

## Put the Mower to Bed

- Run the gas out, or fill the tank and add fuel stabilizer.
- Clean the grass gunk off the deck.
- Pull the blade and get it sharpened over the winter. [Is my blade dull?](/blog/sharpen-your-mower-blade/)
- Battery mower? Bring the battery inside. Cold kills them.
- Full checklist: [the DIY tune-up](/blog/lawn-mower-maintenance-diy/).

## Over the Winter

Stay off frosty or frozen grass when you can. Footprints on frozen blades break them and leave brown tracks in spring. Then rest up. [The off-season program](/blog/lawn-off-season-training/) starts now.
