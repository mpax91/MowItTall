Lime is the most guessed-at product in the garage. Every fall, somebody's dad spreads two bags "because you're supposed to." Sometimes he's right. Sometimes he's been raising the pH of a lawn that was already fine for 15 years. Don't be somebody's dad. Test first.

## What Lime Actually Does

Lime raises soil pH. That's the whole job. When soil is too acidic (sour), your grass can't get at the food that's already in the ground. It's like a fridge full of groceries with the door locked. Lime is the key.

It is **not** fertilizer. It doesn't green anything up next week. It doesn't kill weeds. It works slowly on the soil over months.

## Do You Need It?

Get a [soil test](/blog/lawn-soil-test/). It costs about as much as a pizza and takes 20 minutes. Then:

| Your pH | What to do |
|---|---|
| Below 5.5 | Yes, lime. Probably two rounds. |
| 5.5 to 5.9 | Yes, lime. Your test tells you how much. |
| 6.0 to 7.0 | No. You're in the sweet spot. Walk away from the bag. |
| Above 7.0 | Absolutely not. Lime makes it worse. |

Not sure what your report says? Plug the numbers into my [soil test translator](/blog/lawn-soil-test/). It'll tell you how much lime per round for your lawn size.

## How Much Lime Per 1,000 Square Feet

- **Use your test's number.** Labs factor in your soil type. Clay is stubborn and needs more. Sandy soil needs less.
- **Typical range: 20 to 50 pounds per 1,000 square feet.**
- **Never more than about 50 pounds per 1,000 square feet at once.** More than that sits on the surface and can be rough on the grass. Split big doses: half in fall, half in spring.
- **Do the math once.** Lawn size ÷ 1,000 × the rate = pounds. A 5,000 square foot lawn at 40 pounds per 1,000 is 200 pounds, or five 40-pound bags. Don't know your size? [Measure your lawn](/measure/) on the map.

## Which Kind of Lime

- **Calcitic lime:** the default. Raises pH, adds calcium.
- **Dolomitic lime:** raises pH and adds magnesium. **Only use it if your test says magnesium is low.** If magnesium is already high, stick with calcitic.
- **Pelletized:** regular ground lime squished into pellets so it goes through a spreader without a dust cloud. Same pounds needed. I use pellets.
- **"Fast-acting" lime:** smaller bags, smaller doses, wears off faster. Fine for a tweak, not a real fix.

## How to Put It Down

1. **Fall is best.** Freezing and thawing all winter works it in. Early spring is fine too.
2. **Use a spreader.** Half the rate, two passes in a crisscross. [My spreader method](/blog/spreader-settings-half-rate-twice/).
3. **Water it in** or let rain do it.
4. **Retest in about a year.** Then every few years after that.

## The Bottom Line

Test. Lime only if pH is under 6. Fall, 50 pounds max per round, retest next year. That's the whole thing. Your dad can keep his two bags.
