Most lawns around here get plenty of rain in spring and fall. Watering is mostly a July and August question.

## Is It Actually Thirsty?

- **Footprints stay.** Walk across it. If the grass doesn't spring back in a few minutes, it's dry.
- **Blue-gray color.** Thirsty grass turns a dull blue-gray before it turns brown.
- **Folded blades.** The blades curl up to save water.
- **The screwdriver test.** If a screwdriver won't push 6 inches into the soil easily, it's dry down deep.

My [Should I water?](/resources/should-i-water-my-lawn/) tool does this math for you: rain in the last week versus what the lawn used, plus the forecast.

## How to Water the Right Way

1. **Go deep, not often.** About an inch a week total, in one or two sessions. Deep water pulls roots down.
2. **Early morning.** Before 10 a.m. is best.
3. **Measure once.** Put a few empty tuna cans on the lawn and time your sprinkler. Now you know your number forever.
4. **Count the rain.** An inch of rain this week? You're done. A cheap rain gauge pays for itself.
5. **Watch for runoff.** If water runs down the driveway before you hit half an inch, split it into two shorter runs an hour apart.

## New Seed Is Different

New grass seed has no roots yet, so it needs the top inch of soil kept damp. That means short, light watering one to three times a day until it sprouts, then slowly switching to the deep-and-less-often plan over a few weeks. [Overseeding guide](/guides/when-to-overseed-lawn/).

## Sprinkler System Tips

- Run zones in the early morning, not at dusk.
- Add a rain sensor or a smart controller so it skips rainy days.
- Shady zones need a lot less than sunny ones.
- Turn it off once the grass stops growing in the fall.
