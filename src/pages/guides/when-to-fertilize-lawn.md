Cool-season grass (bluegrass, ryegrass, fescue) does most of its root building in the fall. That's when food matters most. In spring it grows like crazy on its own.

## The Schedule

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Early to mid April | Crabgrass preventer (not food) | Only if you get crabgrass |
| Late May | Light feeding, slow-release | Most lawns |
| Early July | Half dose, only if you water | Optional |
| Early September | **The big one** | Everyone |
| Mid-October | Second fall feeding | Most lawns |
| Mid to late November | Last feeding before the freeze | Optional |

The fun version, with a countdown to the next holiday: [the holiday fertilizer schedule](/blog/holiday-lawn-fertilizer-schedule/).

## Why Fall Wins

After the summer heat, grass shifts to making roots and storing energy. Feeding in September and October means a thicker lawn by Halloween, a faster green-up next spring, and fewer weeds because there's no room for them.

## How Much

- **Per feeding:** about 1 pound of nitrogen per 1,000 square feet, max. Less is fine.
- **Per year:** about 2 to 3 pounds total if you leave your clippings on the lawn (they're free fertilizer).
- **Not sure what that means on the bag?** The [bag decoder](/blog/how-to-read-a-fertilizer-bag/) turns the three numbers into "how far does this go."

## Tips That Save Money

- **Get a soil test first.** If your pH is off, the grass can't use the food anyway. [Soil test guide](/blog/lawn-soil-test/).
- **Leave the clippings.** They return a good chunk of what the lawn needs. [Mulching vs bagging](/blog/mulching-vs-bagging/).
- **Sweep it back.** Fertilizer on the driveway washes into storm drains. Blow or sweep it back onto the grass.
- **Don't feed frozen or soaked ground.** It just runs off.
