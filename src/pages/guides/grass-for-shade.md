Grass is a sun plant. Trees are shade machines. Every shady lawn is a negotiation, and the trees have more lawyers. Here's how to get the best deal.

## The Shade Grasses

- **Fine fescues** (hard fescue, creeping red fescue, Chewings fescue, sheep fescue): the most shade-tolerant cool-season grasses. Fine blades, low fertilizer needs, prefer drier soil. Hard fescue is one of the best performers.
- **Turf-type tall fescue:** handles moderate shade. Needs at least about 4 hours of direct sun.
- **Kentucky bluegrass:** the least shade-tolerant of the bunch, except a few shade-specific varieties.
- **Rough bluegrass:** grows in cool, constantly wet shade, but I'd call it a weed in a normal lawn ([it's on my weed board](/weeds/rough-bluegrass/)).

**Bottom line:** under trees, buy a shade mix that's mostly fine fescue. [Pick your grass in 4 taps](/resources/what-grass-seed-should-i-buy/) and it'll tell you the mix and the pounds.

## How to Care for a Shady Lawn

1. **Let in light.** Trim the lower branches. Even an hour more sun helps.
2. **Mow tall:** 3.5 inches or higher. Shady grass needs every bit of leaf it has.
3. **Go easy on fertilizer.** Fine fescue doesn't want much, and too much hurts it.
4. **Don't overwater.** Fescues in shade would rather be dry than wet. Wet shade invites disease and [moss](/guides/moss-in-lawn/).
5. **Rake the leaves fast in fall.** A blanket of leaves on shade grass is lights out.
6. **Keep traffic off.** Shade grass is thin and slow to recover.
7. **Overseed every fall.** Shade lawns thin out a little every year. [Overseed in September](/guides/how-to-overseed-lawn/), early in the month.

## When to Quit

If you've seeded three Septembers in a row and it's still thin, the tree is winning, and that's OK. A wide ring of mulch under the tree looks better than a sad patch of dirt and grass. Shade groundcovers or moss work too. A moss patch is green, soft and never needs mowing. Some of the best-looking shady yards in the Northeast gave up on grass on purpose.
