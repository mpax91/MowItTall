The right mower depends on how much grass you have, not how much lot you have. Measure the lawn itself first: [draw it on a map](/measure/) and you'll have the square feet in about a minute.

## The Chart

| Lawn (actual grass) | Best fit | Typical deck |
|---|---|---|
| Under 5,000 sq ft | Push mower, battery mower or robot | 20–21 in |
| 5,000–10,000 sq ft (≈ quarter acre) | Self-propelled walk-behind or robot | 21–22 in |
| 10,000–22,000 sq ft (≈ half acre) | Self-propelled walk-behind, wide walk-behind or robot | 21–30 in |
| Half an acre to ≈ 2 acres | Lawn tractor | 42–48 in |
| 2+ acres, or big open lawns | Zero-turn | 48–54+ in |

An acre is 43,560 square feet. A "quarter-acre lot" is about 10,900, but after the house, driveway and beds, the lawn is usually much less.

## What Changes the Answer

- **Hills:** go smaller and walk-behind on anything steep. Sit-down mowers, especially [zero-turns](/guides/zero-turn-vs-riding-mower/), have slope limits.
- **Gates:** measure your narrowest gate before you buy. A 42-inch deck doesn't fit through a 36-inch gate.
- **Trees and beds:** more obstacles means a narrower deck or a zero-turn. A wide tractor deck means more trimming.
- **Storage:** a tractor needs a garage bay. A walk-behind needs a corner.
- **You:** if you like mowing (and the [workout](/blog/mowing-the-lawn-is-a-workout/)), a walk-behind on half an acre is a great Saturday. If you hate it, [a robot](/blog/is-a-robot-mower-worth-it/) might be your answer.

## Whatever You Buy

It has to [mow at 3.5–4 inches](/guides/how-high-to-mow-grass/) and mulch the clippings. Everything else is comfort. For the walk-behind decision, see [self-propelled vs. push](/guides/self-propelled-vs-push-mower/), and for every mower type in one place, [walk-behind vs. riding vs. zero-turn](/blog/walk-behind-vs-riding-vs-zero-turn-mower/).
