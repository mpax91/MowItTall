Aerating pulls little plugs of soil out of the lawn. That lets air, water and fertilizer reach the roots, and it gives grass seed a perfect spot to land. It's great before overseeding. It's not magic every year.

## Do You Need It? Quick Test

- **Screwdriver test:** push a long screwdriver into moist soil. If it's a fight to get it in a few inches, the soil is packed.
- **Puddles** that sit after a normal rain.
- **High traffic:** the kids' soccer zone, the dog's racetrack, the path to the shed.
- **Thick thatch:** more than about half an inch of brown spongy layer between the grass and the soil.
- **You're overseeding.** Aerating is the best way to get seed touching soil.

None of these? Skip it this year.

## When

- **Best:** September, right before overseeding.
- **OK:** early spring, but it can break up a crabgrass preventer barrier. Aerate first, then apply the preventer.
- **No:** summer heat, frozen ground or soggy soil.

## How to Do It Yourself

1. **Water the day before** (or wait for rain). Moist soil plugs better. Dry soil won't let the tines in.
2. **Flag your sprinkler heads,** invisible dog fence wires and anything shallow.
3. **Rent a core aerator** from a hardware or rental store. They're heavy, so bring a friend and a truck, or split one with a neighbor. Or hire it out; lots of lawn services offer it in fall.
4. **Go over the lawn twice,** the second pass at an angle to the first.
5. **Leave the plugs.** They melt back in.
6. **Seed and feed right after** if you're overseeding. [Overseeding guide](/guides/when-to-overseed-lawn/).

## Aeration vs. Dethatching

Dethatching rips out the dead layer with blades. Aerating pulls plugs. For most lawns, aerating plus mowing tall and [leaving clippings](/blog/mulching-vs-bagging/) keeps thatch in check without a dethatcher.
