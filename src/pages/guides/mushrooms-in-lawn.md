You wake up after three rainy days and your lawn has grown a village of mushrooms overnight. Relax. Your lawn isn't sick. It's composting.

## Why They Show Up

Mushrooms are the fruit of fungi living in the soil. Those fungi eat buried wood, old tree roots, thatch and other organic matter. After a stretch of rain (or an overenthusiastic sprinkler), they pop up. When it dries out, they vanish. The fungus is still down there, quietly turning old roots into lawn food.

Common spots: where a tree was cut down, over buried construction scraps, and in shady, damp corners.

## Do They Hurt the Grass?

Almost never. The exception is **fairy rings**: circles or arcs of mushrooms, extra-green grass, or sometimes a ring of brown grass, spreading outward from a buried food source. They're mostly cosmetic. Aerating the ring and watering it deeply can help the grass along.

## What to Do

1. **Mow or rake them off** and bag them. Done.
2. **Water less** if you have a sprinkler system. [Settings that make sense](/guides/how-long-to-run-sprinklers/).
3. **Grind out old stumps** and dig out big buried roots if the same spot keeps sprouting.
4. **Rake out thick thatch** in spring.
5. **Skip the fungicide.** It doesn't really stop them, and you'd be treating a lawn that isn't sick.

## The One Rule: Kids and Dogs

Some mushrooms are poisonous if eaten, and the safe ones and the bad ones can look alike. If kids or dogs use the lawn, pick mushrooms as soon as you see them and put them in the trash. If anything gets eaten, call your vet or poison control. More in the [kid and pet safe lawn care playbook](/blog/kid-and-pet-safe-lawn-care/).
