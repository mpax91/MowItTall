A robot mower follows the same rules as any mower. Mow tall, mow often, leave the clippings. It just does it every day without being asked. The settings that matter change with the seasons.

## Spring (March–May): Wake It Up

- **First cut:** if the grass got shaggy before the robot came out, cut it once with a regular mower (or have it done). Robots handle a little growth every day, not a hayfield.
- **Height:** set it to the top, 3.5 inches or as close as your model goes. Leave it there all season.
- **Hours:** May and June are peak growing season. Give it more run time now than any other part of the year.
- **Check the blades.** Those little razor blades dull fast. Start the season with a fresh set.
- **Walk the lawn** for winter debris: sticks, dog toys, the pinecone it will absolutely find.

## Summer (June–August): Back Off

- **Height:** still at the top. Tall grass shades the soil and handles heat.
- **Hours:** cut back when the grass slows down in the heat. A robot running every day on a crispy, dormant lawn just wears paths.
- **Water:** if you water, go deep about an inch a week in the early morning, and schedule the robot so it isn't running on soaked turf. [Should I water this week?](/resources/should-i-water-my-lawn/)
- **Edges:** trim them yourself every week or two. That's the deal you made.

## Fall (September–November): Leaves and the Last Runs

- **September:** grass grows fast again. Bump the hours back up.
- **Seeding?** Keep the robot off new seed until it's been mowed a few times by hand. Use the app's no-go zones.
- **Leaves:** robots choke on heavy leaves. Pause it during big drops, mulch the leaves with a regular mower or rake them off, then let it back out.
- **Last runs:** when growth slows in late October and November, drop it one notch, to about 3 inches, for its final few runs. Shorter going into winter means less matted grass and snow mold.

## Winter (December–February): Put It to Bed

1. Clean the deck, wheels and blades.
2. Charge it the way the manual says for storage.
3. Store it inside, somewhere dry and above freezing.
4. Check the manual about the charging station: some can stay out, some should come in.
5. Sharpen your patience. It's back in April.

## Every Season: Safety

Run it when the lawn is empty: no kids, no dogs, no toddlers chasing it. And run it in daylight. Researchers who collision-tested 18 robot models found that some caused extensive damage to hedgehog carcasses and others caused none. Whatever model you have, night is when the wildlife comes out.
