If you only change one thing about your lawn, make it this. Most bad lawns I see around Westchester aren't sick. They're just mowed too short.

## Why Taller Grass Looks Better

- **It blocks weeds.** Crabgrass seeds need sunlight on the soil to sprout. Tall grass keeps the soil shaded.
- **It grows deeper roots.** The roots go about as deep as the grass is tall. Deep roots find water in August when short lawns go brown.
- **It stays thicker.** Each blade has more leaf to make food, so the lawn fills in on its own.

## The Right Height by Grass Type

| Grass | Mow at |
|---|---|
| Kentucky bluegrass | 3 to 4 inches |
| Perennial ryegrass | 3 to 4 inches |
| Tall fescue | 3.5 to 4 inches |
| Fine fescue (shade) | 3 to 4 inches |

Not sure what you have? Most Northeast lawns are a mix. Just use 3.5 to 4 inches and you're right for all of them. My [grass guide](/blog/grass-types-northeast/) explains each one.

## The One-Third Rule

Never cut off more than a third of the blade in one mow. If you mow at 4 inches, mow again before the grass hits 6. In May that can mean twice a week. In a dry August it might be every 10 days.

Missed a week and it's a jungle? Don't hack it down in one go. Cut it to 5 inches, wait a few days, then go to 4.

## How to Set Your Mower

1. Park on a flat driveway and turn the mower off. Pull the spark plug wire or take out the battery.
2. Measure from the ground to the bottom edge of the blade.
3. Set every wheel to the same height. One low wheel scalps a stripe down the lawn.
4. Most mowers top out around 4 inches. Use the highest or second-highest notch.

## When to Go Lower (and How Low)

- **Last mow of the fall:** drop to about 2.5 to 3 inches over your last two mows, so the grass doesn't mat down under snow. [Here's when to stop mowing](/guides/when-to-stop-mowing/).
- **Right before you overseed:** cut to about 2 to 2.5 inches so seed can reach the soil. [Overseeding guide](/guides/when-to-overseed-lawn/).

That's it. Every other week of the year: tall.
