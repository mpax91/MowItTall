Grubs are the C-shaped white larvae of beetles (Japanese beetles, European chafers and a few others). They live in the soil and eat grass roots. A few are normal in every lawn. A lot can kill patches of it.

Here's the thing the grub control commercials skip: research in New York found most lawns never have enough grubs to matter. Lots of people treat every year for nothing.

## Step 1: Is It Grubs?

Grub damage shows up from **late August through October** (and sometimes in spring) as:

- Brown patches that **peel back like a rug**, because there are no roots holding them down.
- **Animals digging** at night: skunks, raccoons, crows and turkeys tearing up chunks of turf.

Brown patches that don't lift easily are usually something else: drought, dog spots, disease or a dull blade. My [What’s wrong with my lawn?](/resources/#dx) tool helps sort it out.

## Step 2: Count Them

1. In late summer, pick 3 or 4 spots, including the edge of any brown patch.
2. Cut three sides of a 1-foot square with a shovel, about 2 inches deep, and fold the turf back.
3. Count the grubs in the soil and roots. Put the turf back and water it.

| Grubs per square foot | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Nothing. Rest easy. |
| 5 to 10 | Probably nothing if the lawn is healthy and watered. Watch it. |
| 10 or more | Worth treating that area. |

## Step 3: Pick the Right Timing

- **Prevent next year (late spring to July):** preventive products go down before beetle eggs hatch in midsummer, then get watered in. Only worth it if you had real damage this year.
- **Kill grubs that are there now (mid-August to late September):** curative products work while grubs are small and close to the surface. Water before and after so they're near the top.
- **The natural option (mid-August to October):** beneficial nematodes, tiny worms that hunt grubs. Apply in the evening to moist soil and keep it damp.
- **Too late (late October to May):** grubs are deep in the soil for winter. Most products won't reach them.

Always follow the product label for rates and watering.

## Step 4: Fix the Damage

Rake out the dead grass and overseed the patches in September. [Overseeding guide](/guides/when-to-overseed-lawn/). A thick, deep-rooted lawn mowed tall can lose some roots to grubs and never show it.
