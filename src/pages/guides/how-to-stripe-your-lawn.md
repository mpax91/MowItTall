I’m known around Bedford as the lawn guy because of the stripes. Here’s the whole secret: **it’s not paint and it’s not a special grass. It’s light.**

## How Do Lawn Stripes Work?

Every blade of grass is a tiny mirror. When the mower pushes it over, it leans the way you were walking.

- **Grass leaning away from you** shows you the flat, shiny side of the blade. Looks **light**.
- **Grass leaning toward you** shows you the tips and shadow. Looks **dark**.

Walk to the other side of the lawn and the light and dark stripes swap. Go check. It’s the most fun you can have on a lawn without a mower running.

## How to Stripe Your Lawn, Step by Step

1. **Mow tall.** Set the mower to 3.5 to 4 inches. More blade, more bend, better stripes.
2. **Do the edges first.** Mow two laps around the outside of the lawn. That’s your turning lane, called a header strip.
3. **Pick a target.** Choose something straight to aim at: the sidewalk, the driveway, the side of the house. Your first stripe sets up every other stripe.
4. **Mow your first stripe dead straight.** Look at your target, not at the mower.
5. **Turn in the header strip and come back.** Overlap the last stripe by a few inches so you don’t leave a skinny strip of tall grass.
6. **Repeat until you’re done.** Every pass goes the opposite way of the last one.
7. **Finish with one more edge lap.** It cleans up the scuffs from your turns.

## How Do I Make Stripes Darker?

- **Add a roller.** Mowers with a rear roller stripe best. For other mowers, a striping kit (a roller or a weighted rubber flap behind the deck) does the same job.
- **Mow when it’s dry.** Wet grass clumps and smears the pattern.
- **Keep the blade sharp.** A clean cut looks better and the grass stands up evenly.
- **Double-cut for a party.** Mow each stripe twice in the same direction. The bend gets deeper.
- **Feed it.** Thick, healthy grass has more blades to bend. The <a href="/blog/holiday-lawn-fertilizer-schedule/">holiday feeding schedule</a> is all you need.

## Which Grass Stripes Best?

| Grass | Stripes | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kentucky bluegrass | **Great** | Soft blades that bend and reflect |
| Perennial ryegrass | **Great** | Shiny blades, the classic ballpark stripe |
| Fine fescue | Good | Soft and fine, bends easily |
| Tall fescue | OK | Stiff, wide blades bend less |

Want the best stripes? A bluegrass and rye mix. See <a href="/blog/grass-types-northeast/">every grass, ranked</a>.

## Patterns Beyond Straight Lines

Straight stripes come first. After that you can do diagonals, checkerboards, diamonds and the spiral. They all use the same rules, just more passes. I put a picker with how-to steps for each one in <a href="/blog/lawn-stripe-patterns/">the pattern post</a>.

## Mistakes That Ruin Stripes

- **Mowing short.** Scalped grass has nothing left to bend.
- **Same direction every week.** You get ruts and grass that leans one way for good. Switch it up every week or two.
- **Sloppy turns.** Spinning a mower on the lawn tears up the turf. Turn wide in the header strip, or do a three-point turn.
- **Staring at the mower.** Look ahead at your target. Your stripe goes where your eyes go.
