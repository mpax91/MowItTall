A bare spot is a symptom. If you don't fix the reason it died, the new grass dies too.

## Step 1: Why Did It Die?

| Clue | Likely cause | Fix first |
|---|---|---|
| Round spot, green ring around it | Dog pee | Water the spot after the dog goes, or train a pee zone |
| Turf peels up like carpet | Grubs | [Check for grubs](/guides/grubs-in-lawn/) |
| Along the driveway or street | Road salt or heat | Flush with water in spring, use salt-tolerant seed |
| Under a tree | Too much shade | Fine fescue, trim branches, or mulch it |
| A worn path | Foot traffic | Stepping stones, or tall fescue |
| Where something sat | Pool, bouncy house, kiddie pool | Just reseed |

More causes and fixes: the [damage report](/blog/dog-pee-kids-bouncy-houses-lawn/).

## Step 2: Patch It

1. **Clear it.** Rake out dead grass and debris.
2. **Loosen the soil.** Scratch up the top inch with a metal rake or hand cultivator. Hard soil = dead seed.
3. **Add a thin layer** (about a quarter inch) of compost or topsoil if the soil is poor or the spot is low.
4. **Spread seed** that matches your lawn, at about the new-lawn rate on the bag. [Pick your grass](/lawn-101/#grass).
5. **Press it in.** Step on it or tamp it with the back of the rake so seed touches soil.
6. **Cover lightly** with a thin sprinkle of straw or peat moss to hold moisture. You should still see soil through it.
7. **Starter fertilizer** on the patch, at the bag rate.

## Step 3: Keep It Alive

- **Water lightly once to three times a day** so it never dries out, until the new grass is up and growing. [Watering guide](/guides/how-often-to-water-lawn/).
- **Keep feet and dogs off** for a few weeks.
- **First mow** when the new grass reaches about 4 inches.
- **Watch the timer:** my [seed countdown](/resources/can-i-seed-now/#seedwatch) tells you when to expect sprouts.

## Timing

Late August through September is best. Spring patches work, but new grass faces summer heat soon after, and most crabgrass preventers stop seed too, so don't put a regular preventer on patched spots.
