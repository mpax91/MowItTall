Same blade, same deck, same cut. The only question is whether you push it or it pulls you.

## Push Mower

**Best for:** small, flat lawns, roughly a quarter acre or less.
**Why:** cheaper, lighter, fewer parts to break. You control the speed with your legs.
**Skip it if:** you have hills, a bigger lawn, or you bag. Pushing a full bag uphill builds character, but not that much character.

## Self-Propelled

**Best for:** bigger lawns, any real slope, or anyone who bags clippings.
**Why:** the wheels do the pushing; you just walk and steer. Same cut, less sweat.

### Which Wheels Drive?

- **Rear-wheel drive:** the pick for hills and bagging. A full bag puts weight over the rear wheels, so it grips better as it fills.
- **Front-wheel drive:** the pick for flat lawns with lots of turns. Push down on the handle, the front lifts, and it pivots easily.
- **All-wheel drive:** the pick for steep, bumpy or uneven yards. Costs more.

## Gas or Battery?

- **Battery:** quiet, no gas cans, push-button start, almost no maintenance. Best for small to medium lawns. Check runtime against your lawn size and price out a second battery.
- **Gas:** more runtime and power for big lawns and thick grass. Needs fresh gas, oil and a [spring tune-up](/blog/lawn-mower-maintenance-diy/).

## What to Look For Either Way

- **A deck height that goes to 3.5–4 inches.** If it can't [mow tall](/guides/how-high-to-mow-grass/), don't buy it.
- **Mulching.** You want to [leave the clippings](/blog/stop-bagging-your-clippings/).
- **An easy blade swap.** You're going to [sharpen it](/blog/sharpen-your-mower-blade/). (Own two.)
- **A rear roller or striping kit** if you're one of my people.
