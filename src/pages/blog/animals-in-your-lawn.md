  - q: "What does it mean when skunks dig in your lawn?"
    a: "They’re hunting grubs or earthworms. Small cone-shaped holes that show up overnight are the classic sign. It doesn’t always mean you have grubs, so peel back a square foot of sod in a few spots and count before treating."
  - q: "Do moles mean my lawn has grubs?"
    a: "Not necessarily. Moles mostly eat earthworms, so they usually mean you have healthy, moist soil full of worms. Grub killer often won’t get rid of them."
  - q: "Are bees in my lawn a bad sign?"

**Every animal in your yard is a clue.** Skunk holes usually mean bugs in the soil. Moles usually mean worms, which is good. Geese mean your grass is too short. Your lawn can’t talk, but the wildlife snitches on it constantly.

So let’s read the room. Find the clue, tap the card.

## The big takeaways

- **Digging animals are hungry, not evil.** Skunks, raccoons, moles and birds dig for food. Figure out what food, then decide if you care.
- **Check before you buy grub killer.** Peel back a square foot of sod in a few spots and count. Lots of people treat for grubs they don’t have.
- **Some visitors are compliments.** Worms, moles and bees mean your soil is alive and you’re not nuking everything. Take the win.
- **Tall grass fixes more than you think.** It shades out weeds, saves water, and geese hate it.
- **Your dog is the only one you can train.** Maybe.

**Bottom line:** before you declare war on the wildlife, read the clue. Then [take the quiz](/plan/) and fix the lawn, not the raccoon.
