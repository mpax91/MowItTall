**I love trees. Trees are beautiful. Trees give us shade, oxygen and property value. And every October, some of them try to ruin my life.** Here’s the official power ranking of the worst trees for fall leaves, from saint to menace. Tap your tree below to see where it landed and what to do about it.

## The power ranking

**8. Honeylocust (MVP).** Tiny leaflets fall and vanish into the grass like they were never there. Light shade all summer. If you’re planting a tree near a lawn, this is the dream.

**7. Ginkgo (one and done).** Turns gold, then drops everything in about a day. One mow and your season is over. The female trees drop fruit that smells like a gym bag, but that’s a different ranking.

**6. Pine (overrated villain).** Gets blamed for everything. Drops needles slowly all year. The “pine needles make your soil acidic” thing is mostly a myth. Rake them into your beds and move on.

**5. Silver maple (just a lot).** The leaves mulch beautifully. There are just so many of them. Then in spring it rains helicopters, and half of them try to become trees in your lawn.

**4. Sycamore (too much).** Leaves the size of a pizza box, peeling bark and seed balls. They cover the lawn fast and turn into a wet mat if you blink.

**3. Black walnut (chaos).** Drops its leaves early, which feels nice. Then it starts throwing baseballs at you. Walnuts dent mower decks and stain everything they touch.

**2. Sweetgum (menace).** Gorgeous leaves. Then the gumballs: spiky little ankle-breakers that roll under your feet, jam the mower and keep coming for months.

**1. Oak (dead last).** Here we go. Tough, leathery leaves that take forever to break down. They drop in waves from October to December. Some oaks hold their dead leaves all winter, just to drop them on your clean lawn in April. Plus acorns. Millions of acorns. Every squirrel in Westchester has a timeshare in your yard. **The oak is the guest who won’t leave the party. Oaks are going down.**

## What to do with all of it

Same answer for almost every tree: **mulch the leaves with your mower.** Chop them until you can see grass through the bits. Do it once a week through the season. Oak leaves need two passes. Pick up nuts and gumballs first so they don’t become missiles. [The full mulching guide](/guides/should-i-mulch-leaves-into-lawn/).

Too thick? Chop once, then move the extra into your garden beds. Free mulch. And for the love of everything, [you never needed a leaf blower](/blog/you-never-needed-a-leaf-blower/).

## The bottom line

Honeylocust is a saint. Oak is a villain. You can’t pick your trees, but you can pick your weekend: mow the leaves into the lawn and go watch football.
