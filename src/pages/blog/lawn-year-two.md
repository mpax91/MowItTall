**Year one is fixing. Year two is tuning.** If you made it through a full lap of the lawn calendar, the hard part is done. Here's what changes now.

## Less Fighting, More Growing

The habits from year one compound. A lawn mowed tall, fed in fall and seeded in September gets thicker, and a thick lawn crowds out weeds on its own. Most people find year two has fewer weeds to chase and more mowing to do. That's the good kind of problem.

## Do These in Year Two

1. **A soil test** if you skipped it. You know what you're working with, so you stop guessing. [How to do it](/blog/lawn-soil-test/).
2. **Overseed again in September.** Thin spots take a couple of falls to fill in all the way. [Step by step](/guides/how-to-overseed-lawn/).
3. **Measure the lawn** if you never did, so every bag you buy is the right size. [On a map](/measure/).
4. **Sharpen the blade,** again. (Own two.)

## Should You Level Up?

Every effort level adds a little more work for a little more lawn.

- **Couch Potato → Good Enough Guy:** add Columbus Day feeding and a crabgrass preventer around Tax Day.
- **Good Enough Guy → Curb Appeal Hunter:** add Memorial Day feeding and a spring cleanup.
- **Curb Appeal Hunter → Lawn Psycho:** July 4th (only if you water), Thanksgiving, aeration and a lot of opinions.

Every extra feeding means extra mowing, so only go up if you'll keep up. [See every level on the calendar](/calendar/).

## Or Don't

Plenty of great lawns run the same simple plan every year. Mow tall, leave the clippings, feed on Labor Day, seed in September. That's not settling. That's the system working.
