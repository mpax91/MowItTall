**Hire out the mowing if it buys your weekend back. Keep the fertilizer and weed spraying for yourself. Those programs follow one schedule for every yard on the route, and you can do it cheaper, and better, with a spreader and 15 minutes.**

Before we start: yes, hiring a lawn service means turning in your man card. There’s a form. Your neighbor has to sign it. The guy at the hardware store keeps a copy.

I’m kidding. Mostly. Let’s actually look at it.

<div data-widget-slot></div>

## Mowing: The One You Can Hire With Your Head Held High

Paying someone to mow is paying for time. If you hate mowing, have a huge yard, or have a life, that’s a fair deal. Nobody gets a trophy for suffering.

But if you hire it, be the boss of it:

- **Tall.** 3.5 to 4 inches. Crews love to mow short because it buys them an extra few days between visits. Short grass is how you get weeds and a brown August.
- **Leave the clippings.** That’s free fertilizer.
- **Sharp blades.** Ask. Watch their face.

Here’s my bias, though: mowing is the good part. It’s 45 minutes, you see the stripes come in, and you’re outside. If you can, keep it.

## The Spray Program: Here’s the Problem

The “5-step program” sounds scientific. Here’s what it actually is: a truck, a route, and a calendar. Step 3 happens to your yard the same week it happens to every yard on the street, whether your lawn needs it or not.

- **One schedule for everyone.** Your shady backyard, the sunny corner lot, the new build with no topsoil: same visit, same product, same day.
- **Bought in bulk, built for the route.** They buy by the truckload, and their products are picked to work okay on any lawn, not great on yours.
- **No customization.** Nobody checked your soil. Nobody looked at your grass type. Nobody asked about the dog.
- **Stuff you might not need.** Grub control every year “just in case.” Weed spray across the whole lawn for six dandelions.

For what a season of visits costs, you can buy the same kinds of products for a long time. And you get something the truck never gives you: you learn your lawn.

## DIY Is Cheaper, and It’s Better

When you do it yourself, you get to experiment. Try a fall feeding. Spot-spray just the weeds you have. Seed the thin spot by the driveway. See what works. Next year, do more of that.

That’s how every great lawn you’ve ever driven past got that way. Not a program. A person paying attention.

Here’s the whole DIY program, and it’s short:

1. **Get a soil test.** Once. Now you know what you’re working with. [How to do one](/blog/lawn-soil-test/).
2. **Feed on three holidays.** Memorial Day, Labor Day, Columbus Day. [The schedule](/blog/holiday-lawn-fertilizer-schedule/).
3. **Spot-spray weeds** in fall when they show up. Not the whole lawn.
4. **Mow tall.** Free.
5. **Seed thin spots** in September.

That’s it. One Saturday morning a month, tops.

## When Hiring Actually Makes Sense

- **Core aeration.** Heavy machine, once a year at most. Hiring it (or renting with a neighbor) is reasonable.
- **A real mystery.** Weird spots spreading? A one-time diagnosis beats a yearly contract. Your county extension office helps too.
- **You physically can’t.** Totally different conversation. Hire whatever you need.

## The Bottom Line

Keep your man card. Hire the mowing if you want your Saturdays back. Fire the program, buy a spreader, and learn your own lawn. It’s cheaper, it’s better, and the first time a neighbor asks who does your lawn, you get to say “me.”
