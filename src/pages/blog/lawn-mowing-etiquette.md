People ask me this more than they ask about fertilizer. Not “how do I get stripes.” “Is 7:45 on a Saturday OK?”

No. It’s not. Here are the official rules. They aren’t the law. They’re worse. They’re what your neighbors think of you.

*Your town may have its own noise rules. If it does, those win. These are the manners on top.*

## The Cheat Sheet

- **Weekdays:** 8am to 8pm
- **Saturday:** 9am to 7pm
- **Sunday:** 10am to 6pm
- **Holidays:** mow the day before
- **Neighbor’s party:** never
- **Battery or electric mower:** 30 minutes of grace on each end

Not sure? Ask the machine.

## How Early Is Too Early?

**8am on a weekday.** People are making coffee, getting kids out the door, and taking work calls from the kitchen. Your mower can wait until the bus leaves.

**9am on Saturday.** Saturday morning is the one morning a week nobody has to be anywhere. Some of your neighbors are sleeping. Some are sleeping off Friday. You’re about to wake up both groups at once.

**10am on Sunday.** Sunday morning is the single most complained-about mowing slot in America. I made that up, but you know it’s true.

And don’t be the first engine on the street. If it’s 8:05 and the block is silent, start in the backyard, on the side farthest from the houses.

## How Late Is Too Late?

**8pm on weekdays, 7pm on Saturday, 6pm on Sunday.** After that, people are eating dinner outside, putting kids to bed, or sitting on the porch pretending the week didn’t happen. Nobody wants engine noise with their pasta.

Also: **if you need headlights, go inside.** You can’t see your stripes in the dark, so what are we even doing.

## Weekdays vs. Weekends

Weekdays get the longest window because most people aren’t home. Weekends get shorter windows, because everyone is home and outside, and that’s exactly when your mower annoys the most people.

The sweet spot: **Saturday from 10 to 4.** Everyone is up, half the street is already mowing, and nobody can say a word.

## Holidays

The rule is simple: **mow the day before.**

Memorial Day, the Fourth, Labor Day: the whole point is a cookout, and every yard on the street has guests. You want the stripes fresh *before* the burgers, not during. (Three of those are also [feeding days](/blog/holiday-lawn-fertilizer-schedule/). Same deal: do it the day before.)

If you absolutely have to mow on the day, it’s a quick cut, front yard first, done by noon. After noon, you’re not mowing. You’re competing with somebody’s toast.

## Your Neighbor Is Having a Party

Never. There is no exception.

Not “just the front.” Not “real quick, it’ll take ten minutes.” Not “they won’t even hear it.” They will hear it. Their guests will hear it. Somebody’s uncle will say “who mows during a party?” and everyone will look at your house.

Mow the day before, wait until the cars leave, or, wild idea, go to the party.

## Bonus Rules

- **Point the chute at your own lawn,** not the road and never their driveway. (They’re free fertilizer anyway. [Leave them on the lawn.](/blog/stop-bagging-your-clippings/))
- **If someone is talking to you, kill the engine.** Nodding and pointing at your ear while the mower runs is not a conversation.
- **Wave at people walking by.** You’re the guy with the nice lawn. Act like it.
- **Your landscaper counts as you.** If the crew shows up at 7am on a Saturday, your neighbors aren’t mad at the crew. [Text your guy.](/blog/what-to-tell-your-landscaper/)
- **Robot mowers follow the same rules.** They’re quiet, but running one overnight is how toads and bunnies get hurt. Set it to daytime. ([More on robots.](/blog/robot-mower-judging-you/))

**Bottom line:** mow when it’s light out, when people are already up, and when nobody next door is lighting a grill for guests. That’s it. That’s the whole etiquette.
