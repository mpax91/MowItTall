**Seed, compost and plain fertilizer are low-worry with kids and pets, as long as you water them in and keep the bag away from the dog. Weed and bug killers are where you slow down: read the label, keep everyone off until it’s dry, and spot-treat instead of blanketing the yard.** And here’s the part nobody selling you a bag will say: you don’t need any of them to have a great lawn.

Chemicals are a tool. They’re effective and efficient. They are not a requirement. You can get to “nicest lawn on the street” a bunch of different ways, and some of them don’t involve anything you’d need to hide from a toddler.

Here are your options, from zero chemicals to the full treatment.

## The Best Play Is Offense

If you only read one section, read this one. **Thick grass is the best weed killer ever invented.** Weeds are opportunists. They move into bare and thin spots. Take away the empty real estate and they’ve got nowhere to live.

How to play offense:

- **Mow tall.** 3.5 to 4 inches. Tall grass shades the soil, and weed seeds need sun to sprout. Researchers have found that just raising the mower cuts weeds way down, even with no weed preventer.
- **Overseed every fall.** Toss seed on the thin spots every September. Every year the lawn gets thicker and the weeds get evicted a little more.
- **Feed it at the right time.** Hungry grass gets thin. Thin grass gets weeds. [Feed on the holidays](/blog/holiday-lawn-fertilizer-schedule/).
- **Water deep, not often.** Short daily watering grows shallow grass and happy weeds.

The catch? It’s slow. One to three seasons. And one big conflict: **crabgrass preventer stops grass seed too.** If you’re seeding, skip the preventer on those spots. You have to pick one.

## If You Use Anything, the Ground Rules

- **The label is the boss.** It tells you how much, when, and how long to keep people and pets off. Read it every time, even if you’ve used it before.
- **Kids and pets inside** while you spread or spray.
- **Stay off until it’s dry,** or until it’s watered in, whatever the label says.
- **Spot-treat, don’t blanket.** Spray the 12 dandelions, not the whole half acre.
- **Sweep granules off the driveway** and sidewalk back onto the grass, where they belong.
- **No spraying on windy days,** and never near the veggie garden or the sandbox.
- **Lock it up.** Original container, closed, up high. Garage shelves count. Low garage shelves do not.
- **Wash hands and paws** after time on a freshly treated lawn.

## Surprises That Catch People

- **“Organic” fertilizer can be the dog’s favorite snack.** Products with bone meal, blood meal or fish meal smell like dinner. Dogs can eat a lot of it fast, and bone meal can clump into a hard mass in the stomach. Keep those bags sealed and supervise after spreading.
- **Natural doesn’t mean safe.** Strong horticultural vinegar can burn skin and eyes, and it kills grass along with the weeds.
- **Corn gluten meal** is sold as a natural crabgrass preventer. It’s food-grade, which is nice. But real-world results have been hit or miss, it feeds any weeds that do sprout, and it stops grass seed too.
- **Weed and feed means weed killer everywhere.** You’re treating every square foot to get the few weeds you have.
- **Clover plus bare feet plus bees.** If you let clover grow, mow before the kids run around, or get them shoes.
- **Lawn mushrooms.** Most are harmless, some aren’t. If you have little kids or a dog who eats everything, kick them over and toss them.

## The Low-Worry List

- Grass seed
- Compost
- Plain lawn fertilizer, watered in
- Pelletized lime (way less dusty than the powder)
- Mowing tall and watering deep
- A weeding tool and a podcast

## Grubs, Without the Heavy Stuff

Got grubs? First, make sure. Peel back a square foot of sod in a few spots and count. A few is normal. If you really have a lot, there are gentler routes: beneficial nematodes (tiny worms that hunt grubs, best in moist late-summer soil) and milky spore (Japanese beetle grubs only, and it takes years). Neither is as fast as the standard stuff, but neither needs a “keep off” sign.

## If Someone Eats Something

If someone collapses or has trouble breathing, call 911. Otherwise, don’t panic, and don’t make them throw up unless a professional tells you to. Grab the bag so you can read the label, then call:

- **Kids:** Poison Control, 1-800-222-1222.
- **Pets:** your vet, or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435 (they may charge a fee).

*Not medical or veterinary advice. When in doubt, call.*

**Bottom line:** chemicals are effective, efficient and optional. Play offense, spot-treat if you need to, and follow the label every time. Then [take the quiz](/plan/). There’s an effort level for every family.
