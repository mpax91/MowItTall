**Fertilize on Memorial Day, Labor Day and Columbus Day. Put down crabgrass preventer around Tax Day.** That’s the whole schedule for most lawns around here.

Nobody remembers “when soil temperatures are between 50 and 65 degrees.” Everybody remembers a long weekend. So lawn people tie feedings to holidays. Smart.

The problem is the internet’s version. Some lists have you fertilizing on six holidays: Easter, Memorial Day, July 4th, Labor Day, Halloween and Thanksgiving. That’s a lot of bags. Your lawn doesn’t need it, and neither does your wallet.

## Why three is enough

A lawn like ours needs about 2 pounds of nitrogen per 1,000 square feet a year, as long as you [leave the clippings](/blog/stop-bagging-your-clippings/). A normal bag of lawn food puts down about 1 pound per feeding. Do the math. Three feedings and you’re covered. Six and you’re just paying for extra mowing.

## The holidays

**Tax Day (April 15): crabgrass preventer.** Not a feeding. Pay the IRS, then stop the crabgrass. Tax Day is the reminder, but the real trigger is soil at about 55°F, usually when the forsythia blooms. My [soil thermometer](/) tells you when.

**Memorial Day: light feeding.** The grass is growing fast and summer is coming. Give it a light snack of slow-release fertilizer. Go easy. Too much in spring means more mowing and more disease.

**Labor Day: the big one.** If you only fertilize once a year, this is it. Cool-season grass grows most of its roots in fall. Feed them now and the lawn comes back thick in spring. Bonus: it’s also the best time all year to [seed thin spots](/lawn-101/).

**Columbus Day: the pre-game feed.** I’ve said it before: [Halloween is the Super Bowl of lawns](/blog/halloween-is-the-lawn-super-bowl/). Fertilizer takes a couple of weeks to show. Feed on Columbus Day and your lawn hits its greenest right when the trick-or-treaters show up. Kickoff. Peak lawn. Perfect timing.

## For the Lawn Psychos

You were going to do more anyway. Fine. Here’s how to do it without hurting anything.

**July 4th: half dose, slow-release only.** Only if you water. Never in a heat wave or a dry spell. Cool-season grass is just trying to survive July. Don’t force-feed it.

**Thanksgiving: last call.** One final feeding after the grass stops growing but while it’s still green, before the ground freezes. Then put the spreader away until spring.

## The holidays I skip

**Easter.** Too early. Spring feeding makes the grass grow leaves instead of roots. You mow more, and the soft new growth gets more disease. Wait for Memorial Day.

**Halloween.** You don’t practice during the Super Bowl. The work is done by then. Feed two weeks before, then enjoy the game.

## Spreader rules

- **Look for a 0 in the middle.** The middle number on the bag is phosphorus. Most established lawns don’t need it. Only use it if a soil test says so or you’re starting a new lawn.
- **Follow the bag.** It tells you the spreader setting and how much ground it covers. More is not better.
- **Sweep, don’t hose.** Anything on the driveway or sidewalk gets swept back onto the lawn. Hosing it off sends it down the storm drain.
- **Stay away from water.** Keep it at least 20 feet from streams and ponds.
- **Water it in.** A light rain or a short watering helps it sink in. A big storm washes it away.

Doing all of this sounds like a lot? Just do Labor Day. One feeding at the right time beats four at the wrong time.
