**Under half an acre: walk-behind. Half an acre to two acres: riding mower. Big and open: zero-turn.** Battery and robot mowers? Some people swear by them. I’m not there yet. Here’s the full breakdown, ranked by how much I trust you to be honest about your yard.

## Walk-Behind (Push or Self-Propelled)

My pick, every time.

- **You feel like you did something.** Because you did. [Mowing is a workout](/blog/mowing-the-lawn-is-a-workout/). Tell your doctor.
- **Best stripes.** I get way sharper, straighter stripes walking behind a mower than riding one. You’re closer, you’re slower, you see everything.
- **Cheapest.** By thousands.
- **Safest on hills.** Mow across the slope, not up and down.
- **The catch:** time. A quarter acre takes 30 to 40 minutes. An acre takes your whole Saturday.

**Self-propelled** means the mower pushes itself and you steer. Worth the extra money if you have any hills or more than a small lawn.

**When you buy one, look for:** a 21-inch deck, height that goes to at least 4 inches (tall, remember), a mulching option, and a rear roller if you want stripes. On hills, rear-wheel drive grips better.

## Riding Mower (Lawn Tractor)

- **Saves real time.** Roughly half the time of a walk-behind on a big lawn.
- **There is a cupholder.** I need you to know I know about the cupholder.
- **The catch:** no workout. Expensive. Takes up half the garage. Stripes are harder to get right.

**Look for:** a deck size that fits your gates (42 to 48 inches is common), a good turning radius, and a seat you’d sit in for an hour.

## Zero-Turn

- **Fastest thing on grass.** Spins in place, mows around trees in one move.
- **You look incredible on it.** Not a real benefit. But true.
- **The catch:** priciest. Tricky on hills and slopes. Steering takes some practice. Total overkill on a small yard.

**Look for:** a deck size that fits through your gates, the maker’s slope limit, and a test drive before you buy. Zero-turn steering feels weird for about ten minutes.

## Battery Mowers

Quiet. No gas cans. No pull cord. Push a button and go. Your neighbors at 9 a.m. on Saturday will thank you.

**My take:** not there yet for me. Runtime and extra batteries cost money, and some still struggle in thick, tall grass. But lots of people swear by them, especially on smaller lawns. If your lawn is under about a third of an acre, take a serious look.

## Robot Mowers

They mow a little every day, all by themselves. Your lawn always looks freshly cut. You do nothing.

**My take:** not there yet for me either. They take real setup, they don’t make stripes, you still trim the edges, and you need to keep kids and pets off the lawn while it’s running. Run it during the day, too, since small animals come out at night. But the people who have them really love them.

## The Cheat Sheet

| Mower | Best for | Workout | Stripes | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Push | Small, flat lawns | Yes | Great | $ |
| Self-propelled | Up to ½ acre, hills | Yes | Great | $$ |
| Battery | Small to medium, quiet | Yes | Good | $$ |
| Riding | ½ to 2 acres | No | Okay | $$$ |
| Zero-turn | Big, open, flat | No | Okay | $$$$ |
| Robot | People who hate mowing | No | None | $$$ |
