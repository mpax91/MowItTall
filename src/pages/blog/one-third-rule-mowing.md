**Never cut off more than a third of the grass in one mow.** Keep it at 4 inches? Mow when it hits 6. That’s the whole rule. It’s the one most people break, usually right after vacation.

## Why a Third?

Grass makes its food in the blade. Cut off a little and it barely notices. Cut off half and it panics. It stops growing roots, spends everything it has regrowing leaf, and turns yellow and brown while it recovers. That’s called scalping, and [it’s why half of Bedford is brown in July](/blog/your-landscaper-is-scalping-your-lawn/).

A shocked lawn also makes room for weeds. Thin grass, sun on the soil, crabgrass party.

## The Math, for People Who Hate Math

| You keep it at | Mow when it hits |
|---|---|
| 3 inches | 4½ inches |
| 3½ inches | about 5 inches |
| 4 inches | 6 inches |

## It Got Away From You. Now What?

Back from a week at the beach and the lawn is 9 inches? Don’t drop the mower to 4 and send it. **Step it down.**

1. Raise the mower to its highest setting. Mow.
2. Wait a few days. Lower it a notch. Mow.
3. Repeat until you’re back to normal.

Bag the clippings on that first mow if they’re clumping. [Mulch or bag?](/blog/mulching-vs-bagging/)

## Spring Is When It Gets You

In May, cool-season grass grows like crazy. Once a week might not cut it (pun intended). Every 4 or 5 days in peak spring keeps you inside the rule. In July and November, once a week or less is plenty.

## The Bottom Line

A third at a time. Mow a little more often in spring. And set the mower tall to begin with, so there’s more blade to work with. You don’t need to be a know-it-all. Just mow it tall.
