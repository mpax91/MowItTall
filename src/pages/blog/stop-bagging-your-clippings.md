**Don’t bag your grass clippings. They break down in a few days, feed your lawn for free, and they don’t cause thatch.**

Every Saturday, somebody on my street fills a bag with grass clippings, drags it to the curb, then drives to the store to buy fertilizer.

Friends. That bag *was* the fertilizer.

## What Clippings Actually Do

Grass clippings are mostly water. They dry out and break down in a few days, and the nutrients go right back into the soil. Leaving them on the lawn every time you mow adds up to a meaningful chunk of the feeding your lawn needs for the year. That’s why my [holiday fertilizer schedule](/blog/holiday-lawn-fertilizer-schedule/) can stay light.

## "But They Cause Thatch"

No, they don’t. Thatch is mostly made of tough stems and roots, not soft little clippings. This is one of those myths that just won’t die.

## When to Bag Anyway

- The grass got way too long and the clippings are sitting in clumps on top. (Next time, [mind the 1/3 rule](/blog/one-third-rule-mowing/).)
- You’re collecting them on purpose for compost or the garden.
- The lawn is wet and the clippings are coming out in green clumps. Better plan: wait until it dries.

Otherwise, take the bag off the mower and put it in the garage. ([The full scorecard is here.](/blog/mulching-vs-bagging/)) Your back, your weekend and your lawn all win.

**Small change. Big lawn.** That’s the whole idea around here.
