**Lawn stripes come in five main patterns: straight, diagonal, checkerboard, diamond and the spiral.** Straight is the easiest and looks the best from the street. Checkerboard and diamond take two full passes. The spiral is one long lap that keeps going in. Pick one below and I’ll show you how to mow it.

People ask me about my stripes more than anything else. At parties. At the hardware store. At the gas station, from strangers. So here’s every pattern I mow, ranked by how many cars slow down.

## The Ranking

1. **Diamond.** Two diagonal passes crossing. People pull over. This is the country club look, and it takes about twice as long, which is why you save it for parties.
2. **Checkerboard.** The ballpark. Straight stripes one way, then the whole lawn again the other way. Twice the work, four times the compliments.
3. **The spiral.** Start at the edge, keep going around, end in the middle. It looks like a giant target. Your neighbors will assume you have a plan. You do not. You are just going in circles.
4. **Diagonal.** Straight stripes turned 45 degrees. Looks fancier than it is. Perfect for square lawns.
5. **Straight.** Last on the list, first in my heart. The classic. If you only ever mow one pattern, mow this one, pointed at your front door.

## The Rules for Every Pattern

- **Mow at 3.5 to 4 inches.** Short grass can’t bend, so it can’t stripe. (More on that in <a href="/blog/your-landscaper-is-scalping-your-lawn/">why your landscaper is scalping you</a>.)
- **Edges first.** Two laps around the outside. That’s where you turn.
- **Pick a target.** Aim at something straight. Look at it, not the mower.
- **Overlap a few inches.** Skinny tall strips between passes are the giveaway of an amateur.
- **Dry grass only.** Wet grass smears.
- **Switch it up.** New pattern every week or two, or you’ll wear ruts into the lawn.

## Why It Works

Stripes are just grass bent in opposite directions. Bent away from you, it looks light. Bent toward you, it looks dark. That’s it. No paint, no special seed. A roller behind the mower makes it pop more, and bluegrass and rye stripe best (see <a href="/blog/grass-types-northeast/">every grass, ranked</a>). Proof it works: <a href="/yards/">my yard</a>.
