In Bedford, gas leaf blowers are banned, and people still have strong feelings about it. I’m not here to referee that fight. I’m here to tell you something better.

**Your lawn wants those leaves.**

## Mow them. Seriously.

Run your mower over the leaves when they’re dry. A regular mower chops them into little flakes that fall down between the grass blades. Within a couple of weeks, they break down and feed the soil.

Free fertilizer. No blowing. No raking. No 47 paper bags at the curb.

## The rules

- **Go when they’re dry.** Wet leaves clump and make a mess.
- **Go often.** A few passes through October and November beats one heroic mow on Thanksgiving weekend.
- **Check the grass.** If you can see grass through the chopped leaves, you’re good. If it looks like a brown carpet, mow again or bag that one pass.
- **Rake only the deep piles.** Under a big maple, you may need to move some. That’s it.

## But the neighbors blow theirs into my yard

Then thank them for the free fertilizer and mow it in.

Somewhere on this site there’s a gas leaf blower hiding in a hedge. Go ahead and find it. Bedford would like a word.
