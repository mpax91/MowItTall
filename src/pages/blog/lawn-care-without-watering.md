**You don't need a sprinkler to have a good lawn. You need tough grass, a tall mowing height, fall feeding, and the nerve to let it go brown in August.** Plenty of the best lawns on my street have never seen a sprinkler. Here's the plan.

## Plant the Right Grass

**Turf-type tall fescue** has the deepest roots of the common Northeast grasses, so it finds water when others can't. If you're overseeding a no-water lawn, this is your grass. [Every grass, ranked](/blog/grass-types-northeast/), or [pick yours in 4 taps](/lawn-101/#grass).

## Mow Tall. Like, Tall.

At 3.5–4 inches, grass shades its own roots and the soil stays cooler and wetter. In summer, go to your highest setting and mow only when it's actually growing.

## Feed in Fall, Not Summer

Summer fertilizer on an unwatered lawn is a waste at best and a burn at worst. **Labor Day is the one that matters.** Columbus Day if you want more. [The holiday schedule](/blog/holiday-lawn-fertilizer-schedule/).

## Let It Nap

In a dry July or August, your lawn will turn tan. That's dormancy: the blades shut down, the crowns stay alive. When rain comes back, it greens up in a week or two. [Dead or dormant? The tug test](/guides/is-my-lawn-dead-or-dormant/).

## The Two Times You Should Water Anyway

1. **A long drought.** Grass can usually handle about 4–6 weeks of dormancy. Beyond that, about an inch of water a month keeps it alive. A hose and a sprinkler once every couple of weeks is enough.
2. **New seed.** September seed needs the top inch kept damp until it sprouts. No way around it. [How to water new seed](/guides/how-to-water-new-grass-seed/).

## Don't Do This to a Dormant Lawn

- Fertilize it
- Spray weeds on it
- Park on it, or host a cornhole tournament on it

## The Payoff

A no-water lawn built on deep roots is often tougher than a lawn that's been watered every day. It's just tan for a few weeks a year. Wear it like a tan.
