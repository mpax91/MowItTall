**A robot mower is worth it if your lawn is simple and you hate mowing. It's not worth it if your yard is steep and busy, or if you want stripes.** I don't own one. I mow, because I'm the stripes guy and I have a problem. But I get asked about them constantly, so here's the honest version.

## Why They're Actually Good for Grass

Here's the thing nobody expects: a robot follows the lawn rules better than most people do. It [mows tall](/guides/how-high-to-mow-grass/), it takes a tiny bit off every day (the [one-third rule](/blog/one-third-rule-mowing/) on autopilot), and it leaves the clippings. That's free fertilizer, every day. A lot of robot lawns look great for exactly that reason. [Your robot is judging you](/blog/robot-mower-judging-you/), and it's right.

## Who Should Get One

- **Small to medium lawns** that are mostly open and mostly flat.
- **People who hate mowing**, travel a lot, or would rather spend Saturday literally anywhere else.
- **People who want quiet.** No engine, no Bedford-neighbor side-eye at 8am.

## Who Should Skip It

- **Steep yards.** Every robot has a slope limit. Check it against your steepest spot.
- **Obstacle courses.** Lots of beds, tree roots, play sets and narrow passages mean lots of setup and lots of "help, I'm stuck" notifications.
- **Yards that are always in use.** Kids and dogs out all day means the robot rarely gets a turn.
- **Stripe people.** A robot doesn't stripe. You can stripe on Saturday with a real mower. I won't judge. Much.

## Wired vs. Wire-Free

**Wired:** a boundary wire runs around the edge of the lawn (buried or pinned down). Proven and reliable, but the wire takes an afternoon to install and can get cut by an edger or an aerator.

**Wire-free:** uses satellite positioning, cameras or both to know where the lawn stops. Setup is easier and there's nothing to break, but heavy tree cover can confuse satellite-based models. Lots of Northeast yards have lots of trees, so ask before you buy.

## The Chores Nobody Mentions

- **Edges.** It can't reach right up to the fence or the bed. You'll still trim.
- **Blades.** Little razor blades that dull fast. Swap them on schedule.
- **Leaves.** Robots hate leaf piles. Fall is a manual job.
- **Winter.** Clean it, charge it, bring it inside. ([Settings by season](/guides/robot-mower-settings-by-season/).)
- **Pick-up duty.** Toys, sticks, dog toys, hoses. Every time.

## Safety

Run it only when the lawn is empty, and run it during the day. Collision tests of 18 models found that some did serious damage to hedgehog carcasses and some did none. Night is when the animals are out, so give them the lawn back after dark.

## My Verdict

If your lawn is simple and your Saturdays are precious, a robot can give you a better lawn than you'd give it yourself. If your yard is steep, busy or striped, keep the [walk-behind](/blog/walk-behind-vs-riding-vs-zero-turn-mower/). Either way: mow it tall.
