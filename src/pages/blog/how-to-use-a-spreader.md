**Fill it on the driveway, go around the edge first, then go back and forth at half the bag's setting, and do it again crosswise.** That's the whole technique. Here's everything else nobody tells you the first time.

## Broadcast vs. Drop

- **Broadcast spreader:** throws granules in a fan. Forgiving. Get this one.
- **Drop spreader:** drops granules straight down between the wheels. Precise, but miss by three inches and you'll have a green stripe and a yellow stripe until June.

## Before You Start

1. **Read the bag.** It lists settings for popular spreaders and how many square feet it covers. [How to read a fertilizer bag](/blog/how-to-read-a-fertilizer-bag/).
2. **Know your lawn size,** so you know how much to buy. [Measure it on a map](/measure/).
3. **Fill it on the driveway** with the hopper closed, so spills land on pavement, not grass.

## The Technique

1. **Edge first.** Walk the perimeter once. Use the edge guard or deflector if you have one, so you don't feed the street.
2. **Half rate, twice.** Set it to half the bag's setting. Walk back and forth in straight lines. Then do the whole lawn again at a right angle. Misses get covered by the second pass. [Why I always do it this way](/blog/spreader-settings-half-rate-twice/).
3. **Steady pace.** Walk at a normal speed. Slowing down puts down more.
4. **Close the hopper** every time you stop or turn, or you'll leave a pile.
5. **Overlap a little.** With a broadcast spreader, aim for the edge of the last throw to reach your last wheel track.

## After

- **Sweep the driveway and walk** back onto the lawn. Fertilizer on pavement washes into storm drains.
- **Water it in** if the bag says to.
- **Rinse the spreader** and let it dry, so it doesn't rust or cake up.

## Oops

**Spilled a pile?** Scoop up what you can, then soak that spot with water. Fertilizer left in a pile burns a dead patch.
**Stripes showed up anyway?** Next time, go half rate twice. For now, it'll blend in a few weeks.

Same spreader handles crabgrass preventer, grass seed and lime. One tool, the whole year.
