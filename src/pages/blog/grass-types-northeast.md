Walk into a garden center and there are 40 bags of grass seed. Every one says “Thick! Lush! Miracle!” with a picture of a golf course. Half of them are junk.

Here’s the truth. **Around here, only four grasses make a real lawn: Kentucky bluegrass, perennial rye, turf-type tall fescue and fine fescue.** Everything else is filler or a mistake.

The short version:

- **Sunny and you water?** Bluegrass and rye.
- **Shady?** Fine fescue.
- **You never water?** Tall fescue.

Not sure? Take the four-question picker. It tells you what to buy and how much.

## The cheat sheet

| Grass | Sprouts in | Sun | Water | Spreads? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bluegrass | 2–4 weeks | Full sun | Lots | Yes |
| Perennial rye | 5–10 days | Full sun | Medium | No |
| Tall fescue | 1–2 weeks | Sun to part shade | Little | No |
| Fine fescue | 1–2 weeks | Shade | Little | A little |

Now let’s meet the roster.

## Kentucky bluegrass: the varsity captain

- **Looks:** deep green with a hint of blue. Medium blades with a tip shaped like the front of a boat.
- **Feels:** a thick, soft carpet. The barefoot grass.
- **Sprouts in:** 2 to 4 weeks. You will stare at dirt and think you got scammed. You didn’t.
- **Spreads?** Yes. It sends out underground runners and fills in bare spots by itself. This is its superpower.
- **Sun:** full sun, 6 hours or more.
- **Water:** the thirstiest of the four. In a drought it goes brown and naps, then comes back when the rain does.
- **Traffic:** good, and it heals itself.

**Mike’s take:** the gold standard. Slow starter, great finisher. That’s why it’s almost always paired with rye.

## Perennial rye: my guy

I’ll say it. Perennial rye is my favorite grass. I said what I said.

- **Looks:** the darkest, shiniest green of the bunch. The back of the blade is glossy, and that shine is what makes stripes pop.
- **Feels:** soft and fine.
- **Sprouts in:** 5 to 10 days. Nothing is faster. Seed on Saturday, green by the next weekend.
- **Spreads?** No. It grows in clumps. A bare spot stays bare until you seed it.
- **Sun:** full sun. It’ll put up with a little shade.
- **Water:** medium. It doesn’t love long droughts or hot, sticky summer nights. Water in the morning, never at night.
- **Traffic:** excellent. Sports fields use it for a reason.

**Mike’s take:** it’s the fastest, the darkest and the best striper. The catch: it’s the least winter-tough of the four and it can’t fix its own bare spots, so overseed it every fall. Keep the blade sharp too. A dull blade shreds rye and leaves white tips.

## Turf-type tall fescue: the offensive lineman

- **Looks:** wider blades, a little coarser. The new turf types are way finer than the old pasture stuff, but it won’t pass for bluegrass up close.
- **Feels:** tougher. Not the barefoot grass.
- **Sprouts in:** 1 to 2 weeks.
- **Spreads?** Basically no. It grows in clumps.
- **Sun:** full sun to part shade, about 4 hours or more.
- **Water:** the least of anything in the sun. Its roots can go 2 to 3 feet deep, so it stays green while your neighbor’s lawn turns to toast.
- **Traffic:** excellent.

**Mike’s take:** the “I never water and I have three dogs” grass. One rule: don’t mix it with rye or fine fescue. The blades are too different and it looks patchy.

## Fine fescue: the introvert

Fine fescue is really a little family: creeping red, chewings and hard fescue. They’re usually sold together.

- **Looks:** needle-thin blades. Like grass that skipped leg day.
- **Feels:** the softest of all.
- **Sprouts in:** 1 to 2 weeks.
- **Spreads?** A little. Creeping red spreads some. The others clump.
- **Sun:** the shade champ. Great in dry shade under trees, as long as it gets about 4 hours of direct sun.
- **Water:** very little. Barely wants fertilizer either.
- **Traffic:** poor. It does not want to play football.

**Mike’s take:** if you’ve got trees, this is your grass. Mow it tall, leave it alone, don’t overfeed it.

## The benchwarmers

These show up in cheap bags. Skip them.

- **Annual ryegrass.** Not the same as perennial rye. It grows fast and dies in a year. It’s a summer fling, not a relationship.
- **Rough bluegrass.** Those [lime green patches](/weeds/#rough-bluegrass) that look like a different lawn? Often this. It sneaks into cheap shade mixes.
- **Kentucky 31 tall fescue.** The old pasture version. Wide, clumpy blades. Get “turf-type” or nothing.

## How to read the bag

Flip it over and read the label. This is where the truth lives.

- **The percentages.** The list tells you what’s actually in there. If it’s mostly annual ryegrass, put it back.
- **Weed seed.** Should be close to 0%.
- **Other crop.** Lower is better. That’s grass you didn’t ask for.
- **Named varieties.** Real names beat “VNS” (variety not stated). VNS means they’re not telling you, which tells you something.

## Mixing it up

Most lawns around here are already a mix. When you overseed, match what you have, or grab a blend that fits your sun. Want stripes in the sun? Bluegrass and rye. Got sun out front and shade out back? A sun and shade mix lets each grass win its own spot.

And when do you seed? [Late August through September](/lawn-101/). Fall is the best time all year to grow grass around here. Spring seed fights crabgrass and summer heat. Fall seed gets two cool seasons to grow up.
