**If crabgrass is already up, preventer won't touch it. Either spray it while it's young, or let the first frost kill it and seed over the spots in September.** Then set a reminder for Tax Day. That's the whole post. Here's the long version.

## First: Is It Actually Crabgrass?

Crabgrass is light green, wide-bladed, and grows flat and spread out like a starfish, mostly in sunny spots along driveways and sidewalks. It shows up in early summer and seeds by late summer. If it's a clump of tall, coarse grass in May, it might be something else. [Check the weed board](/weeds/crabgrass/).

## Option 1: Spray It Young (June to Mid-July)

- **Use a post-emergent crabgrass killer for lawns.** The one most homeowners find on store shelves contains quinclorac.
- **Hit it small.** Young crabgrass dies easily. Big, bushy crabgrass laughs at you.
- **Spray on a mild day, under about 85°F,** when it's not about to rain.
- **Plan on a second round** if the label says so.
- **Not on new grass.** If you seeded this spring, check the label's waiting period first.

## Option 2: Let Frost Do It (Late July Onward)

After about mid-July, crabgrass is usually too big to kill easily. It's an annual, so the first frost kills it for free. Meanwhile:

- **Mow tall** so your grass shades it out.
- **Don't fertilize in summer heat.** It feeds the crabgrass more than your grass.
- **In September, rake out the dead clumps and [seed the gaps](/guides/how-to-overseed-lawn/).** A thick lawn is the best crabgrass prevention there is.

## How to Never Be Here Again

1. **Crabgrass preventer around Tax Day.** The real trigger is soil hitting about 55°F, usually when the forsythia blooms. [Timing guide](/guides/crabgrass-preventer-timing/).
2. **Mow at 3.5–4 inches.** Tall grass blocks the sunlight crabgrass seeds need.
3. **Feed in fall, not summer.** [The holiday schedule](/blog/holiday-lawn-fertilizer-schedule/).
4. **Fill bare spots in September,** because crabgrass loves a gap.
