**In a heat wave: mow high and rarely, water deep in the early morning (or not at all), skip the fertilizer and weed killer, and stay off the lawn.** Brown is not dead. Here are the rules.

## Rule 1: Raise the Deck and Mow Less

Tall grass shades its own roots and the soil. Mow at your highest setting, [3.5–4 inches or more](/guides/how-high-to-mow-grass/), and only when it's actually growing. Mow early in the morning or in the evening, not at 2pm. For the grass and for you.

## Rule 2: Sharp Blade, Always

A dull blade tears grass, and torn grass loses water faster in the heat. [Is my blade dull?](/blog/sharpen-your-mower-blade/)

## Rule 3: Water Deep or Not at All

- **If you water:** deep, early in the morning, about an inch a week including rain. [Should I water this week?](/resources/should-i-water-my-lawn/)
- **If you don't:** let it go dormant on purpose. In a long drought, about an inch a month keeps it alive. [How to tell dead from dormant](/guides/is-my-lawn-dead-or-dormant/).
- **Never:** a little every evening. That's a fungus buffet.

## Rule 4: No Fertilizer

Grass isn't growing in a heat wave. It's surviving. Fertilizer now can burn it and mostly feeds the crabgrass. Wait for [Labor Day](/blog/holiday-lawn-fertilizer-schedule/).

## Rule 5: No Weed Killer

Many lawn weed killers can injure grass when temperatures run above about 85°F, and stressed grass is easy to hurt. The weeds will still be there in fall, when spraying actually works better.

## Rule 6: Stay Off It

Dormant, crispy grass breaks under feet, tires and lawn chairs. Move the party to the patio for a week.

## Rule 7: Don't Panic About Brown

A brown Northeast lawn in July is usually asleep. It comes back with the rain. If parts don't, [seed them in September](/guides/how-to-overseed-lawn/). That's the plan anyway.
